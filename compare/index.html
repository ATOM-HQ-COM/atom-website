One question, four answers

“Why does a heavier ball fall at the same rate?”

The four levels inside a class are not four different subjects. They are four different depths on the same question. Here is what each physics tutor does with one prompt (the other four classes work identically).

Archimedes: Heavier things get pulled harder by gravity, but they are also harder to get moving. Those two effects cancel out perfectly, so a bowling ball and a marble hit the ground together. Drop them and see!

Newton: From $F = ma$ with $F_g = mg$, we get $a = mg/m = g$. Mass cancels, so acceleration is $9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ regardless of $m$. On the exam, watch for air resistance: that is the one thing that breaks this.

Heisenberg: The cancellation is the equivalence of inertial mass $m_i$ in $F = m_i a$ and gravitational mass $m_g$ in $F_g = m_g g$. Their equality is empirical, verified by Eötvös-type torsion balance experiments to roughly one part in $10^{13}$.

Einstein: The weak equivalence principle promotes that empirical coincidence to a geometric statement: free fall is motion along a geodesic of a Lorentzian manifold, $\frac{d^2 x^\mu}{d\tau^2} + \Gamma^\mu_{\ \nu\rho}\frac{dx^\nu}{d\tau}\frac{dx^\rho}{d\tau} = 0$. No mass appears in the equation, so no mass dependence can appear in the trajectory.
