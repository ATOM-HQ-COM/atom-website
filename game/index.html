Shift briefing

You have the watch.

  1. Power is set by the rods and the recirculation flow, not by the turbine. Pulling rods out or speeding the pumps up raises power; both take effect through steam voids in the core.
  2. The turbine valves hold pressure, they do not set power. Leave the pressure regulator in automatic until you know what you are doing.
  3. Feedwater keeps the core covered. If the water level falls below the top of the fuel, you are on a clock.
  4. Sell high. The price moves all day. Full power at $180/MWh is worth six times full power at $30, and there are hours when the price goes negative.
  5. Every trip costs you about $1.5 million. The protection system will save the reactor whether you like it or not. It will not save your P&L.
Why this is the hard way to cause a disaster

A reactor is not a bomb waiting for an excuse.

The intuition most people carry is that a nuclear reactor is a barely contained explosion held back by vigilance. The physics says close to the opposite. Almost everything that goes wrong in a power reactor makes the chain reaction weaker, not stronger, and it does so in milliseconds, without electricity, without instructions and without anyone deciding anything.

01

The fuel puts itself out

As uranium heats up, the U-238 in it absorbs neutrons over a wider band of energies. This is Doppler broadening, and it is not a control system, it is a property of matter. In this simulation it is worth about −2.75 pcm per degree: go from full power to 150% and the fuel alone removes roughly 1,200 pcm of reactivity, near-instantly. There is nothing to fail, because there is no mechanism.

02

Boiling is a brake

A boiling water reactor needs its water to slow neutrons down. Raise the power and you make more steam; steam is a thousand times less dense than water, so the moderator disappears exactly where the power went up. The void coefficient here is −0.092 Δk/k, one of the strongest negative feedbacks in engineering. Try to run this plant up and you will feel it fight you.

03

Chernobyl was a different machine

RBMK-1000 had a positive void coefficient at low power and control rods tipped with graphite, so the first metre of insertion briefly added reactivity. That combination does not exist in any Western power reactor and no longer exists anywhere: the remaining RBMKs were rebuilt. Every reactor in this simulation, and every reactor licensed in the West, is required to have negative feedback at every power level.

04

It cannot detonate

A weapon needs uranium enriched above 90% held together by explosives for microseconds. Power reactor fuel is 3-5% enriched, sits in water, and is physically incapable of a nuclear explosion for the same reason wet firewood is incapable of being gunpowder. The Chernobyl explosion was steam, then hydrogen. The Fukushima explosions were hydrogen. Chemistry, not fission.

05

Decay heat is the real problem

Stopping the chain reaction takes three seconds. Removing the 6.5% of rated power that keeps coming from fission products afterwards takes days. Every severe accident in history is a cooling failure after shutdown, not a runaway. That is why this game's meltdown route runs through the water level gauge, not the power meter.

06

Defence in depth is not a slogan

Fuel pellet, cladding, reactor vessel, containment building. Behind them: an automatic scram, relief valves, two independent high-pressure injection systems, low-pressure injection, core spray, containment isolation. To reach INES 7 in this simulation you must switch off five of them and then hold the plant outside its limits for minutes. That is roughly what the record shows: three severe accidents in about 20,000 reactor-years.
